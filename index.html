About

I'm a first-year PhD student in Information Science at the University of Maryland, College Park, working with Prof. Wei. My research sits at the intersection of natural language processing, machine learning, and education. I'm interested in how AI can make classroom practice visible and measurable, and in how far we can trust models when they are asked to judge teaching and learning.

My current work turns the steps of the PACT reading-comprehension approach into coordinated LLM tasks that generate dyslexia-friendly instructional materials. I also build models that recognize instructional activities in classroom video, and I study whether large language models classify students' written summaries consistently with expert categories.

Before the PhD, I earned a B.S. in Information Science (summa cum laude) and an M.S. in Applied Machine Learning, both at UMD. Along the way I've built a Llama-style language model and an English–Chinese translation system from scratch, and I placed third in DrivenData's AI for Advancing Instruction Challenge.

Research

selected projects

PACT+AI: LLM-Assisted Preparation for Dyslexia-Friendly Instruction

Research AssistantJun 2025 – presentLLM systems · Literacy

PACT is an evidence-based approach to teaching reading comprehension. This project operationalizes its steps as coordinated LLM sub-tasks that prepare a lesson: checking whether a text suits the target grade, adapting it when it doesn't, and generating essential words, a comprehension canopy, and model gist statements.

  • Evaluated outputs from prompt ablations against PACT-based checklists to study accessibility and content fidelity.

Multimodal Classification of Instructional Activities

3rd placeAIAI Challenge, DrivenDataJun – Aug 2025

Framed classroom-activity recognition as second-by-second multi-label classification over instructional video, where activity codes overlap and some are rare. Built a training pipeline with an imbalance-aware loss that improved macro-F1.

Classroom Video Analysis

Research AssistantOngoingComputer vision · Multimodal

Methods for turning recordings of real lessons into data researchers can analyze, without exposing the confidential video itself.

  • Extracting whiteboard content from lab-recorded classroom video to support downstream analysis of teacher behavior.
  • Detecting teacher behaviors in Zoom recordings of online teaching, using a manually annotated codebook as ground truth.
  • A parsing pipeline that converts ELAN (.eaf) annotations into second-level ground-truth tables for model evaluation.

LLM Classification of Student Gist Statements

Research AssistantOngoingLLMs · Educational assessment

After reading a short passage, students write a one-sentence gist. This project tests whether large language models sort those statements into the categories and subcategories defined by education experts, and whether their errors cluster on particular classes.
